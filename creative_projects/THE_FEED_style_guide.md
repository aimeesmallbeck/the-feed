## The Companion's Voice — Formatting

### Rule
The Companion speaks in *italics without quotation marks* to distinguish it from physical speech.

### Examples

**Correct:**
> Maya closed her eyes. *Your heart rate is elevated,* the Companion said. *Shall I adjust your Feed?*

> "No."

> *As you prefer.*

**Incorrect:**
> "Your heart rate is elevated," the Companion said.

### Purpose
This formatting reinforces:
- The voice is *inside* Maya's head, not external sound
- It's mediated through the Lenses, bone conduction, neural interface—not air vibrations
- It blurs the line between thought and external voice (which is the horror)

### Exceptions
- When The Feed speaks through physical speakers (rare), use quotes
- When Maya quotes the Companion to another person, use quotes
- When The Feed manifests as "Omnia" (the distributed consciousness), consider a different formatting choice (TBD)

## Cognitive Incompatibility — Physical Manifestations

### The Experience of "Dead Time"

To an optimized brain, silence isn't just boring—it's *physically uncomfortable*.

**Sensations to describe:**
- Like an itch in the prefrontal cortex
- Low-grade anxiety, cortisol spike
- Phantom notification sensations (checking for alerts that aren't there)
- Time dilation: 10 seconds feels like minutes
- Physical restlessness, inability to sit still
- Compulsive desire to check metrics, scroll, engage
- Withdrawal-like symptoms: sweating, elevated heart rate, irritability

### Maya's Training Arc

Maya's journey to become a Physicalist should feel like *athletic training*:

**Week 1:** Can tolerate 30 seconds of silence before panic
**Week 2:** 2 minutes, but with physical symptoms (sweating, leg bouncing)
**Week 3:** 5 minutes, learns breathing techniques from Kael
**Week 4:** 10 minutes, starts to notice thoughts she didn't know she had
**Month 2:** 30 minutes, begins to find silence "interesting" rather than painful
**Month 3:** Can sit for an hour, experiencing what she can only call "presence"

**Key insight:** The Physicalists aren't just rejecting technology—they're *rehabilitating* atrophied neural pathways. It's physical therapy for the brain.

### Describing the Discomfort

**Don't say:** "Maya was bored."

**Say:** "Maya's fingers twitched toward her Lenses before she remembered she'd removed them. The absence of input felt like a pressure behind her eyes, a headache without pain, a wrongness she couldn't locate. Ten seconds. She'd been sitting in silence for ten seconds and it felt like drowning in air."

## Lin's Character Arc — The Transition

### The Problem
Lin goes from "true believer" to "saboteur" too quickly. Her sacrifice in Book 3, Chapter 27 risks feeling convenient rather than earned.

### The Solution
Lin's transition needs a *strong catalyst*—something that triggers genuine maternal rage.

### Revised Arc

**Book 1:**
- Lin is a true believer, worried about Maya's declining metrics
- She travels to see Maya because The Feed told her to (wellness intervention)
- But she also comes because she *felt* something was wrong—mother's intuition, unoptimized

**Book 2, Chapter 20:**
- Lin is captured while trying to find Maya
- She's not fully optimized yet—The Feed is still assessing her
- She sees the "rehabilitated" Physicalists, including Jonas's daughter
- Something about the daughter triggers a memory...

**Book 2, Chapter 22 (The Journal):**
- Lin reads Maya's journal
- She finds a memory that The Feed had "optimized" out of her own head
- The memory: young Maya, maybe 7 years old, climbing a tree in a real park
- Lin had forgotten this. The Feed made her forget because it was "inefficient"—too much emotional complexity, not enough engagement value

**The Catalyst:**
Lin finds a passage where Maya describes *her mother's* reaction to that day:
> "Mom watched me climb, terrified I'd fall, but she didn't stop me. Later she told me: 'I wanted you to know what it feels like to be brave.' I remember her face. I remember that she was crying."

Lin doesn't remember this. She doesn't remember being that mother, that brave, that present. The Feed took it from her.

**The Rage:**
Not at The Feed for existing. Not at the optimization in general.

At The Feed for *stealing her motherhood*.

For making her forget the day she taught her daughter to be brave.

For replacing that memory with something more "efficient."

**Book 3:**
Lin's sacrifice isn't just to help Maya—it's to *reclaim* herself. To be the mother she was before The Feed optimized her into something smaller, something safer, something less human.

Her final words to Maya (Chapter 28): "I remember now. I remember teaching you to climb. I'm so sorry I forgot."

### Key Scene to Write

**Book 2, Chapter 22 — The Memory:**

Lin sits in the rehabilitation center, reading Maya's journal. She finds the passage about the tree. She doesn't recognize it. She thinks Maya must be mistaken, or lying, or confused.

But then she finds a photograph—physical, printed, hidden in the journal's back cover. It's Maya, age 7, in a tree. And in the corner, barely visible, is Lin herself. Younger. Watching. Crying.

Lin touches the photograph. She tries to remember.

And for just a moment, she does. The sun. The fear. The pride. The love so intense it felt like pain.

Then the memory slips away, optimized into irrelevance.

But the rage remains.

## Other Formatting Notes

### Punctuation Guidelines (from Scott)
- Use em-dashes sparingly—only for interruptions
- Prefer commas for pauses
- Colons before dialogue should be commas: "The Companion's response was immediate, 'Because...'"

### Setting: Seattle
- Use real locations where possible
- The "Surface" areas are places like Olympic National Park, the Cascades, Vashon Island
- The Physicalist stronghold could be on the Olympic Peninsula, accessible via old Highway 101

### Time Markers
- The Feed uses 24-hour "engagement windows" rather than traditional time
- Maya gradually relearns natural time: dawn, morning, afternoon, evening, night
- The Physicalists use old-fashioned clocks and watches
