# The Consensus Universe

**Status:** Concept / Seeking Source  
**Inspiration:** Unidentified short story (possibly 1970s-80s, possibly Sheckley/Lafferty/Bester tradition)  
**Core Appeal:** Reality as consensus, physics as belief system, evolution of understanding

---

## The Premise (As Remembered)

The universe creates itself as humanity's understanding evolves.

- Before fire: The universe is small, simple, limited
- Discovery of fire: Expands the possible
- Flat earth era: The world is literally flat
- Heliocentrism: The solar system becomes real
- Each scientific discovery doesn't just describe reality — it *creates* it
- Modern physics: The fully fleshed-out universe we know now

**The implication:** We didn't discover the universe. We *built* it through understanding.

---

## The Core Concept

**Reality as emergent property of consciousness.**

Not "belief shapes reality" (magic) but "understanding creates the possibility space" (physics as collective project).

**The twist:** What happens when we stop understanding? Or when different groups understand differently?

## Potential Modern Angles

### 1. The Fragmentation
**Premise:** In the internet age, consensus breaks down. Different communities "understand" different physics. Reality begins to fragment.

**The story:** A physicist notices experimental results are becoming inconsistent. Different labs get different results. It turns out the flat-earthers, the quantum-consciousness mystics, the simulation theorists — they're all *right* in their own bubbles. Reality is local now.

**The horror:** You can drive from one physics to another. The borderlands are... unstable.

---

### 2. The Last Unexplored Place
**Premise:** We've mapped everything. Understood everything. The universe is "complete." And it's dying.

**The story:** As human knowledge approaches completeness, the universe loses its capacity for surprise. Stars begin to dim. Quantum effects freeze. The protagonist is a "deliberate ignorant" — someone who refuses to learn, to keep something unexplored, to keep the universe alive.

**The question:** Is a complete understanding worth a dead universe?

---

### 3. The Discovery of the Previous Civilization
**Premise:** We find evidence that another intelligent species existed before us. They understood the universe differently. Their version of physics starts to overwrite ours.

**The story:** Archaeologists find alien mathematics. As they decode it, local physics shifts. Their math *works*. Our math starts to fail. The alien understanding is older, more fundamental, more "real."

**The stakes:** Can we maintain our version of reality against a competing consensus?

---

### 4. The Child Who Doesn't Know
**Premise:** A child grows up isolated, without being taught physics. Their personal reality is... different. And it's *stable*.

**The story:** Raised in a closed environment, the child develops their own physics. It works — for them. When they're introduced to the wider world, their reality and ours collide. The child's physics is simpler, more malleable, more powerful.

**The dilemma:** Do we teach them "correct" physics and destroy their power? Or let them rewrite our world?

---

### 5. The Physics of the Dead
**Premise:** What did the recently deceased "understand" just before death? And what reality are they now maintaining?

**The story:** Near-death experiences aren't hallucinations — they're glimpses of the physics the dying person believes in. The afterlife is real because enough people believe in it. But different people believe in different afterlives. The dead are fragmenting into incompatible realities.

**The protagonist:** Someone who can communicate with the dead, navigating between their competing physics.
