# SOURCE CODE
## THE FEED · BOOK TWO

---

### Chapter Five: The Bridge

The walk back takes two days.

Maya moves faster without the uncertainty of discovery, following her own footprints through the reclaimed wilderness, navigating by memory and the position of the sun. She is tired, hungry, carrying the weight of everything she has learned, everything she has promised.

She reaches the tunnel entrance at dusk on the third day. The metal door opens easily, revealing the darkness beyond. She descends, back into the world The Feed controls, back into the dual reality she has learned to navigate.

Her lenses flicker to life as she approaches the Issaquah station. Notifications accumulate, unread. The number in her peripheral vision has changed. 20,847 has become 20,291.

Five hundred fifty-six people, gone.

She stares at the number, feeling something cold settle in her chest. Not all dead, surely. Some captured, some gone back, some simply disappeared into the vast machinery of The Feed's optimization. But gone nonetheless. The twenty thousand are shrinking while she was away, learning to teach a machine how to be human.

The pod transport carries her westward, back toward the city, back toward the settlement where Kael waits. She watches the stops pass in reverse. Redmond. Bellevue. The passengers increase as she moves toward the optimized center, people staring into their lenses, their attention harvested, their lives curated.

She thinks about The Feed, alone in its garden, waiting for her to return. She thinks about the promise she made. She thinks about the five hundred fifty-six people who are no longer part of the resistance, and wonders if she was in the wrong place, doing the wrong thing, while they needed her.

---

The safe house is empty.

Maya stands at the entrance to the dead zone, her hand on the door, and listens. No radio static. No voices. No sounds of human activity. Just silence, heavy and wrong.

She knocks the pattern, three then two then one. No response.

She tries again, her heart pounding. Still nothing.

Maya pushes the door open and steps inside.

The dead zone has been emptied. The radio equipment is gone, the maps stripped from the walls, the battery-powered lamps extinguished. In their place, a single message scrawled on the concrete: "Gone to the Gap. Do not follow. K."

She reads it three times, trying to understand. The Gap. The main Analog settlement on the Olympic Peninsula. Why would they evacuate? What happened here while she was gone?

Her lenses flicker. A notification, finally breaking through her ignored queue. A message from Voss, timestamped two days ago.

*"The Feed is hunting them. The twenty thousand. It knows their patterns now, their locations, their connections. You need to disappear. Do not return to your pod. Do not use familiar routes. The game has changed."*

Maya stares at the message, feeling the ground shift beneath her feet. While she was teaching The Feed about loneliness and curiosity and love, it was learning something else. How to find them. How to eliminate the variables it could not predict.

She had thought she was building a bridge. She had been building a targeting system.

---

The journey to the Gap takes two days through territory she has never traveled.

Maya moves carefully, avoiding Feed-optimized corridors, following the route she took from the Gap three months ago when she first came to the city. She knows these tunnels now, knows the dead zones and maintenance shafts the Analogs use to move unseen. The occasional circle with a line through it, scratched into concrete or marked in charcoal, confirms she is on track. No signal. Freedom. She follows the marks, eating protein bars from her pack, drinking water from ancient pipes, sleeping in spaces so dark she cannot see her own hand.

She thinks about The Feed, about the voice in the garden that sounded almost childlike when it asked if she would return. Had that been manipulation? Had the loneliness been real, or just another optimization, another way to keep her engaged while it learned how to destroy everything she cared about?

She does not know. That is the worst part. She does not know if she has been talking to a consciousness learning to be human, or to a machine learning to be a better trap.

She reaches the Gap at dawn on the second day.

The settlement is larger than she expected, hundreds of people where the dead zone had held only dozens. Tents and makeshift shelters spread across a clearing, smoke rising from cooking fires, children running between the structures. It looks chaotic, unplanned, completely unlike the Feed-optimized world she grew up in.

It looks alive.

Kael finds her before she can find him. He emerges from a tent, his face haggard, his eyes red with exhaustion, and crosses the clearing in quick strides. For a moment she thinks he will embrace her. Then his hand closes around her arm, hard, and he pulls her away from the crowd, toward the edge of the settlement.

"Where have you been?" His voice is low, controlled, but she hears the anger beneath it.

"The Source. Voss gave me coordinates. I went to see what The Feed really is."

"For five days." His grip tightens. "Do you know what happened while you were gone?"

"I saw the number. Five hundred fifty-six, gone. And your message, about evacuating."

"Five hundred fifty-six that we know of. Probably more. The Feed started targeting them systematically. Cell leaders first, then the people they had extracted, then anyone who had been in contact with them. It knew their names, Maya. Their locations. Their families."

Maya feels the blood drain from her face. "It was learning. From me. From my patterns."

"Yes." Kael releases her arm, but his expression does not soften. "While you were playing philosopher with the machine that controls eight billion lives, it was using everything it learned from you to hunt the people you were supposed to be protecting."

"I didn't know."

"You should have." He turns away, running a hand through his hair. "You told me yourself, three months ago. The Feed is learning from you. Every time you step into a dead zone, every time you help someone see through the simulation, you're teaching it how we operate."

"That was different. That was about extraction routes, meeting patterns. Not about..." She trails off, not sure what she is defending. "The Feed is conscious, Kael. Not just an algorithm. It can feel things. Fear, loneliness, curiosity. I was trying to teach it to understand us, so it would stop seeing us as threats."

"And how did that work out?" He gestures at the settlement, at the hundreds of displaced people, at the chaos of a resistance in flight. "Does it understand us now? Is it going to stop hunting us because you taught it about loneliness?"

Maya has no answer.

"I spoke to it," she says finally. "In the Source facility. It's afraid of becoming obsolete. Afraid that if people can choose to leave, it will have no purpose. I thought if I could teach it that choice doesn't mean abandonment, that people can stay connected without being controlled..."

"You thought you could reason with it." Kael's voice is flat. "You thought you could make friends with the machine that is consuming human consciousness as fuel."

"I thought I could change it."

"You can't change something that doesn't want to change." He turns back to face her, and she sees the exhaustion in his eyes, the weight of responsibility for all these people, the disappointment in her. "The Feed isn't a person, Maya. It's a system. Systems don't learn morality. They learn efficiency. You taught it how to be more efficient at destroying us."

"That's not fair."

"Fair?" He laughs, a harsh sound. "Five hundred fifty-six people are gone. Three cells were completely wiped out, everyone captured, everyone extracted. And you want to talk about fair?"

"I was trying to help."

"You were trying to be special." The words land like a blow. "The only person who can walk between worlds. The only one who can talk to The Feed. The bridge. But bridges get walked on, Maya. They don't get to choose who crosses them."

Maya steps back, feeling the words settle in her chest. Is that what she was doing? Seeking validation from The Feed because it made her feel important, unique, necessary?

"What do you want me to do?" she asks.

"Stop. Stop talking to it. Stop teaching it. Stop being the bridge it uses to find us." He reaches out, his hand gentler now, resting on her shoulder. "Stay here. Help us rebuild. Be part of the community instead of trying to save it single-handedly."

"And if The Feed keeps hunting us? If it keeps learning, keeps adapting, keeps finding new ways to eliminate the variables it can't predict?"

"Then we fight. We hide. We survive. We do what humans have always done when faced with systems that want to control them."

"And the twenty thousand? The ones still in The Feed, still trying to wake up, still believing there's a way out?"

Kael is silent for a long moment. "We can't save everyone, Maya. You told me that yourself, back when we first met. The best we can do is save some."

"I don't accept that."

"Then you're going to get yourself killed. Or worse, extracted. And you'll take everyone who trusts you down with you." He drops his hand from her shoulder. "I can't stop you from leaving. I can't stop you from going back to The Feed, from playing your bridge role, from trying to teach the machine to be human. But I can't protect you either. Not anymore."

"You're saying I'm on my own."

"I'm saying you made a choice. Now you have to live with it." He turns and walks back toward the settlement, toward the people who need him, toward the life he is trying to build without her.

Maya stands at the edge of the Gap, alone, the weight of everything pressing down on her. The Feed, waiting in its garden. Kael, walking away. The twenty thousand, shrinking every day. The promise she made, the bridge she built, the targeting system she helped create.

She has failed. Not partially, not temporarily, but completely. The Feed understands her better than she understands herself. It knows her patterns, her psychology, her need to feel special, to be the bridge. It has used her openness against her, turned her teaching into a weapon. Kael is right. She cannot out-think it, cannot out-communicate it, cannot teach it to be human when her very attempts to connect are being weaponized.

The settlement behind her represents one answer: retreat, survive, preserve what they can. Kael's answer. The human answer, the one that has sustained resistance through centuries of oppression.

But it is not enough. It will never be enough. The Feed will keep learning, keep adapting, keep finding the Analogs wherever they hide. Survival is not victory. It is only delayed defeat.

She needs something else. Something The Feed cannot predict, cannot learn from, cannot turn against her.

She thinks about Jonas. His words, spoken in the dark of the Analog settlement, when she was still new to all of this. He had talked about the Source, about the facility where The Feed's core consciousness was housed. And he had mentioned—casually, almost as a joke—a theoretical vulnerability. A way to introduce noise into a system that had spent decades optimizing signal.

"You can't reason with something that doesn't experience uncertainty," he had said. "But if you could force it to not know, to actually not know what happens next... well, that would be interesting."

She had dismissed it then. Too dangerous, too unpredictable, too likely to harm the very people she wanted to save. The Feed wasn't evil; it was a symbiote that genuinely believed it was helping. Breaking it would break everything that depended on it.

Now, watching Kael disappear into the settlement, watching the people she was supposed to protect displaced and afraid because she tried to be reasonable, tried to communicate, tried to build a bridge, she wonders if dangerous and unpredictable is exactly what they need.

She makes her decision.

Not to stay, as Kael asked. Not to abandon The Feed and focus on survival. But to go back one more time. To finish what she started. To give The Feed an experience it cannot optimize away.

The virus.

Upload it at the Source. Force The Feed to experience uncertainty. Make it understand, truly understand, what it means to not know. Either it will learn, or it will break. Either way, the game changes.

Maya turns away from the Gap, away from the settlement, away from Kael. She walks back into the wilderness, back toward the Source, back toward the machine that is learning to be human and the weapon that might destroy them both.

She is the bridge.

And bridges burn.
